Four things differ, the rest is common sense

Most inventory apps are either too light (a notes app) or too heavy (a professional WMS). This project solves the four problems an individual or small shop actually runs into.

1 Three levels, grouped first

Clothing → Tops → T-Shirts, Beauty & Care → Skincare → Serum. Items in the same category always stay together and are never split apart by expiry sorting. It sounds simple, but whether the category is the first or second sort key decides whether finding something is fast or slow.

2 Optional expiry, sorted within group

Off by default. Turn it on, enter a production date and shelf life, and the expiry date is computed. Within a group, items with expiry sort by remaining days, expired first. On launch, every expired item is listed.

3 Fully offline, data on device

A local Room database. No account, no cloud sync, no analytics. The network is used only for barcode product lookup; if it fails or you are offline, everything else still works.

4 Used-up items have a home

At zero you can delete the item or keep its record. Kept items collect in a collapsed "Used up" section at the bottom of the list, ready to restore or delete for good — they neither vanish without a trace nor clutter the main list.

Constraints written into the code

These are not marketing lines — they are boundaries not to be broken when changing the code.

  • Every write goes through a transaction: inbound, adjust, undo and category changes are all wrapped in withTransaction(); any failure rolls back completely.
  • Records are append-only: undo does not delete or modify history — it inserts a reversing entry and points the original at it.
  • Expiry is never stored: only the production date and shelf life are kept; the expiry date is computed by SQL.
  • Barcode data is a suggestion only: third-party data can be wrong or stale, so it appears as a card and is written only when you tap "Use".
  • Local beats network: anything you entered yourself (the self-learning cache) is more reliable than any third-party source, so it is queried first.
  • No Google services: ZXing for pure-Java decoding plus a CameraX preview, so devices without GMS (such as HarmonyOS) work the same.

Known limitations

Real boundaries, not "coming soon".

  • Limited barcode coverage: public databases cover roughly 60-70% outside the US and EU. Type it in when lookup fails — the local self-learning cache remembers it after the first time.
  • Single location: designed for personal, household or small-shop use; split items yourself if you keep stock in several places.
  • No system notifications: the expiry reminder appears when you open the app.
  • No cloud sync, no export: switching phones means re-entering data.
